# Core claim

This paper revisits cognitive load theory by centering the relation between limited working memory, long-term memory, schema-based expertise, and instructional design, while also updating how germane processing should be understood.

# Relevance to my project

Use this paper to justify:

- prior knowledge effects through `r_j`,
- goal-relevant processing as a residual or allocated quantity,
- instructional design as a manipulation of processing conditions rather than content alone.
- the idea that information stored in long-term memory changes the effective burden imposed on working memory.

# Constructs and definitions

- `working memory`: limited mainly for novel information,
- `long-term memory`: the store that transforms performance once relevant information is organized and available,
- `intrinsic load`: determined by both information complexity and learner knowledge,
- `extraneous load`: driven by instructional procedures and also analyzable through element interactivity,
- `germane load`: recast as resources redistributed toward intrinsic processing rather than an independent load source.

# Method or formal approach

This is a broad theory integration and update paper rather than a formal model.
Its value lies in clarifying cognitive architecture and revising the interpretation of the load categories.

# Useful evidence

Look for arguments that connect:

- prior knowledge to reduced effective load,
- instructional design to extraneous cost,
- architecture-level constraints to learning outcomes.
- The paper explicitly states that working-memory limitations apply to novel information and effectively disappear when organized information is retrieved from long-term memory.
- It also states that intrinsic load depends on both the nature of information and the knowledge of the person processing it.
- A central update for this project is the claim that germane cognitive load should be treated as a redistributive function of resources from extraneous to intrinsic processing.

# Limitations

The paper gives a strong conceptual scaffold, but still leaves open:

- numerical definitions of the variables,
- the form of the allocation rule,
- and the structural representation of inter-element relations.

It also hints at later extensions such as working-memory depletion, which means it should not be treated as the final word on fixed capacity.
