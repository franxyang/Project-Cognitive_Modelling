# Core claim

Anmarkrud, Andresen, and Braten argue that multimedia-learning research using cognitive load theory often relies on underspecified working-memory concepts, thin subjective load measures, and results that do not consistently align with the theorized relation between cognitive load and learning.

# Relevance to my project

Use this paper to justify the literature-gap part of the proposal:

- working memory is often invoked but underspecified,
- measurement does not always line up cleanly with theory,
- a more explicit formal model is therefore justified.

# Constructs and definitions

- `working memory`: treated as theoretically central but often weakly conceptualized in the reviewed literature,
- `subjective cognitive load measures`: often general and based on one or very few items,
- `theory-measurement mismatch`: a recurring problem in studies that claim to test cognitive load ideas.

# Method or formal approach

This is a critical review of multimedia-learning studies framed by cognitive load theory.
It evaluates how working memory and cognitive load are conceptualized and measured.

# Useful evidence

- The abstract states that most reviewed studies did not clearly conceptualize or measure working memory.
- It also states that many studies relied only on very general subjective load measures.
- The paper further argues that the link between cognitive load theory and mainstream working-memory models appears loose or partly inconsistent.
- For this project, the most valuable use is as evidence that a stronger formal bridge between theory, construct, and measurement is needed.

# Limitations

The paper is a critique of a particular literature, especially multimedia learning.
It diagnoses conceptual and measurement problems well, but it does not by itself provide the formal solution.
