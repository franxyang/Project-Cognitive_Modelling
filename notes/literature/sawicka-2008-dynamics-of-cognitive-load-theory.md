# Core claim

Sawicka argues that the growing complexity of cognitive load theory makes formal simulation useful, and proposes a system-dynamics model that expresses relations among instruction, learner knowledge, working-memory limits, schema development, and the three load types.

# Relevance to my project

Use this paper to show that formalization is already a legitimate move within the cognitive load literature.
It is also the best comparison point for explaining how your project differs:

- you use graph-structured information rather than a generic system-dynamics representation,
- you center an explicit partitioning trade-off,
- and you move more directly toward optimization.

# Constructs and definitions

- `intrinsic load`: tied to the complexity gap between revealed material and learner schemas,
- `extraneous load`: tied to mismatch between required and provided instructional support,
- `germane load`: tied to schema construction and automation,
- `simulation model`: a formal aid for examining learning dynamics under different instructional formats.

# Method or formal approach

This paper uses a formal system-dynamics simulation model.
Its purpose is not point-by-point behavioral prediction, but dynamic analysis of how load and learning evolve over time under different instructional conditions.

# Useful evidence

- The paper explicitly argues that formalization forces precision and can reveal relationships hidden in verbal theory.
- It models cognitive load types together with schema development and working-memory capacity.
- It also argues that simulation can support experimental design by generating dynamic predictions that might otherwise be overlooked.
- For this project, the strongest use is to show that a mathematical treatment of CLT is not a category mistake.

# Limitations

The paper is explicitly illustrative and generic rather than tightly calibrated.
It does not provide:

- a graph representation of information elements,
- a principled partition objective,
- or a direct account of structural coherence loss across segments.
