# Core claim

Kintsch argues that learning from text depends on building a situation model that integrates textual information with prior knowledge, not merely remembering the textbase, and that the optimal level of textual coherence depends on the reader's background knowledge.

# Relevance to my project

This paper is one of the most important additions for justifying why structural preservation matters.
It supports:

- the distinction between remembering pieces of information and learning usable structure,
- the importance of prior knowledge for integrating relations across elements,
- the idea that segmentation or explicitness can help low-knowledge readers but reduce constructive processing for high-knowledge readers.

# Constructs and definitions

- `textbase`: the propositional content explicitly represented from the text,
- `situation model`: a richer representation integrating text with prior knowledge,
- `coherence`: local and global connectedness that supports understanding,
- `zone of learning`: texts need enough overlap with prior knowledge to support learning, but not total redundancy.

# Method or formal approach

This is a theory paper grounded in Kintsch's construction-integration framework and related simulations of high- and low-knowledge readers.

# Useful evidence

- Kintsch explicitly argues that readers may remember a text yet fail to learn from it in a usable way.
- He argues that content overlap with prior knowledge is a necessary condition for learning from text.
- He also argues that low-knowledge readers benefit from coherent, explicit texts, whereas high-knowledge readers may learn better from low-coherence texts that trigger constructive inference.
- This is directly relevant to the idea that reducing local burden can also reduce deeper structural learning.

# Limitations

The paper is about text comprehension and learning from discourse, not a cognitive load model.
It does not provide:

- an explicit capacity-allocation objective,
- a graph-cut or partition cost,
- or a direct instructional optimization rule.
