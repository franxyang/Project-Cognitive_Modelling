# Core claim

Baddeley presents working memory as a system for temporary storage and manipulation that supports complex cognition such as language comprehension, learning, and reasoning, rather than as a passive short-term store.

# Relevance to my project

Use this paper as the classic architectural background for the assumption that processing resources are limited and functionally differentiated.
It is especially useful for supporting the existence of a temporary processing bottleneck behind the model.

# Constructs and definitions

- `working memory`: temporary storage plus manipulation during complex cognition,
- `central executive`: attentional control system,
- `phonological loop`: speech-based maintenance and rehearsal system,
- `visuospatial sketchpad`: visual and spatial manipulation system.

# Method or formal approach

This is a conceptual synthesis paper that combines dual-task, neuropsychological, and individual-differences evidence.

# Useful evidence

- The paper rejects the idea that complex cognition depends on a single unitary short-term store.
- It argues that reasoning, comprehension, and learning cannot be explained well by a simple digit-span storage account.
- It reports that working-memory span is meaningfully related to reading comprehension and reasoning performance.
- It also argues that the phonological loop is important for vocabulary acquisition, which is useful when thinking about how prior knowledge and encoding interact.

# Limitations

This paper gives the classical architecture, but not:

- a flexible resource-rational allocation account,
- a cognitive load theory treatment of instructional design,
- or a formal optimization rule.
