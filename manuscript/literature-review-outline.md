# Literature Review Outline

## Section 1: Why a formal model is needed

Core claim:

The cognitive load literature provides a strong verbal theory of capacity limits and instructional effects, but it still leaves the relation between task structure, processing cost, and learning outcomes under-formalized.

Anchor papers:

- Sweller (1988)
- Sweller (2010)
- Sweller, van Merrienboer, and Paas (2019)
- Anmarkrud, Andresen, and Braten (2019)

## Section 2: Working memory as a limited resource system

Core claim:

Working memory should be treated as a capacity-limited processing resource, and more recent accounts support flexible allocation rather than only fixed-slot language.

Anchor papers:

- Baddeley (1992)
- Ma, Husain, and Bays (2014)
- van den Berg and Ma (2018)

Bridge to the model:

- motivates `C_i`,
- supports optimization and allocation language.

## Section 3: Intrinsic cost as structured information processing

Core claim:

Intrinsic cognitive cost depends not only on the number of information elements, but also on their complexity and the coordination required among them.

Anchor papers:

- Sweller (1988)
- Sweller (2010)
- Chen, Paas, and Sweller (2023)

Bridge to the model:

- motivates node terms and edge terms in `I_i`,
- supports element interactivity as a structural rather than purely scalar property.

## Section 4: Segmentation and representation as a trade-off

Core claim:

Instructional segmentation and representation can reduce local burden while also weakening the coherence of global relational structure.

Anchor papers:

- Sweller, van Merrienboer, and Paas (2019)
- Anmarkrud, Andresen, and Braten (2019)
- Mayer (2024)

Bridge to the model:

- motivates `E_i`,
- motivates the partitioning problem,
- supports a fragmentation penalty like `L(Pi)`.

## Section 5: Coherence, situation models, and structural preservation

Core claim:

Learning requires more than retaining isolated elements.
It depends on preserving a coherent structure that can be integrated with prior knowledge into a usable mental model.

Anchor papers:

- Kintsch (1994)
- Anmarkrud, Andresen, and Braten (2019)

Bridge to the model:

- motivates why `L(Pi)` should matter beyond immediate processing ease,
- supports the distinction between local tractability and global understanding,
- helps justify transfer as more sensitive than recall to connection loss.

## Section 6: Rational compression and allocation precedents

Core claim:

There is already a serious formal literature treating memory under limits as optimization over fidelity, compression, and resource allocation.
Your model should be positioned as extending that logic to structured instructional information.

Anchor papers:

- van den Berg and Ma (2018)
- Nassar, Helmers, and Frank (2018)
- Jakob and Gershman (2023)
- Yoo, Klyszejko, Curtis, and Ma (2018)

Bridge to the model:

- supports optimization under capacity limits,
- supports fidelity versus tractability trade-offs,
- supports strategic resource allocation rather than passive overload language.

## Section 7: Measurement and validation limits

Core claim:

The empirical literature has developed useful subjective and differentiated measures of cognitive load, but measurement validity remains uneven and theory-to-measurement links are still incomplete.

Anchor papers:

- Paas et al. (2003)
- Brunken, Plass, and Leutner (2003)
- Leppink et al. (2013)
- Klepsch, Schmitz, and Seufert (2017)
- Klepsch and Seufert (2020)
- Krieglstein et al. (2022)
- Krieglstein et al. (2023)

Bridge to the model:

- justifies using recall and transfer as downstream outcomes,
- supports cautious use of subjective load measures,
- motivates latent-process thinking.

## Section 8: Formal and computational precedents inside and near the field

Core claim:

Formal modeling is already a legitimate move in adjacent and partially overlapping literatures, even if it remains underdeveloped within mainstream educational cognitive load work.

Anchor papers:

- Sawicka (2008)
- Sohn, Doane, and Jang (2018)
- van den Berg and Ma (2018)
- Jakob and Gershman (2023)

Bridge to the model:

- normalizes simulation and computational formalization,
- helps position the proposal as an extension rather than a category mistake.

## Section 9: Proposed theoretical contribution

Core claim:

The proposed model contributes a graph-structured and optimization-based formalization of cognitive processing under capacity limits, linking intrinsic cost, extraneous cost, and goal-relevant processing to learning outcomes.
