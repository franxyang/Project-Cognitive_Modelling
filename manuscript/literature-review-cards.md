# Literature Review Paragraph Cards

## Paragraph 1: CLT gives the problem, but not yet the formal link

- `claim`
  - CLT correctly identifies limited processing capacity as a central constraint on learning, but it still leaves the relation among task structure, processing cost, and learning outcomes under-formalized.
  - 这一段还要轻微交代 review 的进入路径：从 CLT 基本概念出发，再顺着 working memory、structure、coherence、formalization 往下走。
- `papers to connect`
  - Sweller (1988)
  - Sweller (2010)
  - Sweller, van Merrienboer, and Paas (2019)
  - Anmarkrud, Andresen, and Braten (2019)
- `real tension / limit`
  - The theory is strong enough to motivate the problem, but still too verbal when the key variables need to be linked precisely.
  - The empirical literature often uses CLT language without a clear working-memory model.
- `bridge to next paragraph`
  - If the gap is not “whether capacity matters” but “how to describe it precisely,” the next step is to define working memory as a limited resource system rather than a loose background label.

## Paragraph 2: Working memory should be treated as a limited resource system

- `claim`
  - A formal review needs working memory to function as an allocable and costly resource, not just as a general bottleneck metaphor.
- `papers to connect`
  - Baddeley (1992)
  - Ma, Husain, and Bays (2014)
  - van den Berg and Ma (2018)
- `real tension / limit`
  - These papers support resource language and optimization, but they are not instructional-design models.
  - They explain capacity constraints better than they explain what makes structured learning material expensive to process.
- `bridge to next paragraph`
  - Once working memory is treated as a resource, the next question becomes: what properties of information determine intrinsic processing cost?

## Paragraph 3: Intrinsic cost is about structured interdependence, not raw amount

- `claim`
  - Intrinsic cost cannot be reduced to information quantity alone; it depends on element complexity and on the need to coordinate relations across elements.
- `papers to connect`
  - Sweller (2010)
  - Chen, Paas, and Sweller (2023)
  - Sweller (1988)
- `real tension / limit`
  - CLT identifies element interactivity as the key mechanism, but does not provide an explicit representation of that structure.
  - The theory clarifies what matters, but not how to model it when content is segmented or reorganized.
- `bridge to next paragraph`
  - If cost depends on structure, then segmentation cannot be assumed to help unconditionally; it changes the structure being processed.

## Paragraph 4: Segmentation can reduce local burden and still damage learning

- `claim`
  - Segmentation and instructional support may lower local processing demand, but they can also weaken the global relations learners need to build.
- `papers to connect`
  - Sweller, van Merrienboer, and Paas (2019)
  - Anmarkrud, Andresen, and Braten (2019)
  - Kintsch (1994)
- `real tension / limit`
  - CLT usually emphasizes relief from unnecessary load, while comprehension theory shows that too much explicit support can reduce constructive integration.
  - Lower reported load does not automatically imply better learning.
- `bridge to next paragraph`
  - To make that point defensible, the review has to explain why coherence matters for learning outcomes, especially beyond recall.

## Paragraph 5: Coherence matters because learning is more than retaining pieces

- `claim`
  - Learning depends on building a usable situation model, not only on retaining propositional content, so the loss of cross-element coherence should matter.
- `papers to connect`
  - Kintsch (1994)
  - Anmarkrud, Andresen, and Braten (2019)
- `real tension / limit`
  - Kintsch explains why coherence and prior knowledge matter, but does not give a capacity-based objective for deciding when local simplification is worth structural loss.
  - This leaves the trade-off conceptually clear but formally unresolved.
- `bridge to next paragraph`
  - Once the trade-off is clear, the review can turn to formal models that already treat limited cognition as optimization under constraints.

## Paragraph 6: Formal neighbors already exist, but they solve a different problem

- `claim`
  - Existing formal models show that optimization under cognitive limits is legitimate, but they mostly target memory fidelity, resource allocation, or generic learning dynamics rather than structured instructional partitioning.
- `papers to connect`
  - Sawicka (2008)
  - van den Berg and Ma (2018)
  - Nassar, Helmers, and Frank (2018)
  - Jakob and Gershman (2023)
- `real tension / limit`
  - Sawicka is close to CLT but not graph-structured.
  - The memory models are mathematically stronger, but they do not distinguish intrinsic and extraneous cost or model coherence loss in instructional material.
- `bridge to next paragraph`
  - The final paragraph should state the gap narrowly and defensibly: the issue is not the absence of formal models in general, but the absence of a model for partitioning structured instructional information under capacity constraints.

## Paragraph 7: The real gap is integration

- `claim`
  - The missing contribution is an integrated formal account that combines CLT-style cost distinctions, structural interactivity, coherence preservation, and optimization under capacity limits.
- `papers to connect`
  - Sweller (2010)
  - Kintsch (1994)
  - Sawicka (2008)
  - van den Berg and Ma (2018)
  - Nassar, Helmers, and Frank (2018)
  - Jakob and Gershman (2023)
- `real tension / limit`
  - Current literatures explain different parts of the problem, but they stop short of one model that can handle both local tractability and global structural preservation.
- `bridge to next paragraph`
  - None. This paragraph closes the review and leads into the proposal's model statement.
